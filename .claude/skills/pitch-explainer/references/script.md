# Writing the English script

## Shape of a video (25–50 s)

| # | Chapter | Beat purpose | Example (say) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `<A> VS <B>` / `THE QUESTION` | **Hook**: absurd or provocative question, visible in frame 0 | "Can the worst team in the league beat eleven players who can't see?" |
| 2–3 | `THE PROBLEM` / `THE SETUP` | Rules of the scenario, the viewer's role ("you") | "Your coach yells: take your man." |
| 3–4 | `THE LINEUP` | Top-down shape, names, badge | "They line up in a 4-3-3." |
| 4–7 | `THE SIMULATION` / `THE MATCH` | Play it out: passes, runs, counters climbing | "Nine passes. Zero shots." |
| 8 | `THE TWIST` / `BUT…` | The turn the viewer didn't expect | "But as soon as they lose the ball…" |
| 9 | `THE RESULT` / `THE FIX` | Payoff with a number | "Final score: 4–0." |
| 10 | `PART 2` / none | CTA: follow / comment prompt | "Comment which team should face them next." |

## Rules

- One sentence per beat, 6–14 spoken words, 2–4 s. A new visual beat every
  2–4 s.
- Beat 1 must work muted: the headline *is* the hook question.
- Speak to the viewer: "you", "your man", "your coach".
- Headline ≠ voiceover. It's the 2–6-word punch of the line: line 1 sets up
  (white), line 2 lands (green). E.g. say "He drifts wide and you follow him
  every single time" → title `HE RUNS.` / `YOU FOLLOW.`
- Use concrete numbers (passes, metres, seconds, goals) and put them in `stats`.
- End on a loopable or open note (a question, "part two").
- Plain words; spell numbers in `say` the way they should be spoken
  ("four three three", "four nil") - the headline uses digits.
- Avoid claims about real people that you can't back; keep what-ifs clearly
  hypothetical.

## Tone: hyped + a little Gen-Z

Talk like a hyped young football creator, not a TV pundit. Sprinkle English
youth slang lightly - about one phrase every 2-3 beats, never two in a row:
"bro", "lowkey", "no cap", "nah", "cooked", "built different", "chilling",
"unhinged", "insane", "drop it in the comments". Write them out in full so the
voice can say them ("for real", not "fr"; no "W"/"L"/"fr"/"ngl"). Facts and
numbers stay clear; slang never replaces the information. Titles may borrow one
slang punchline ("BUILT DIFFERENT.", "NOPE. OFFSIDE.") but stay ≤ 5 words.

## Niche ideas (what-if football)

- Can a pro team beat 11 blindfolded players / 11 kids / 22 amateurs?
- What if the offside rule didn't exist?
- What if a team had no goalkeeper for 90 minutes?
- Why man-marking fails / why the offside trap works / how a press works.
- How many passes does it take to get through a low block?
